# Anchoring: controlled interpretation diagnostics

These working diagnostics distinguish established Iridian forms and descriptions from unresolved contextual judgements. A form's presence in the current grammar verifies its morphology and the reading given there; it does not establish its felicity in every context below. No question in the matrices supplies a positive or negative acceptability judgement. English bridging and scope schemas are semantic prompts, not newly licensed Iridian examples.

## Constant child and predicate

The current grammar's constructed paradigm in Chapter 17, `ex:disc-anchoring-controlled`, supplies all three clauses under a constant child-topic interpretation:

| Form | Gloss | Established contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Byl zdrávime. | child.DIR sleep-PROG | Anchoring left unmarked; contextual reference. |
| Si byl zdrávime. | SANCH child.DIR sleep-PROG | Speaker presents a selected child; no assertion of hearer ignorance. |
| Bylo zdrávime. | child.DIR.DEF sleep-PROG | Shared-discourse retrieval of the child. |

Keep the predicate, aspect, case, intended sleeping child, prosody, and topic organisation constant when varying the following identification contexts. Introduce the child as the subject matter independently, so that first linguistic mention does not become a simultaneous test of aboutness-topic licensing.

| Context manipulation | Diagnostic question | Established constraint / unresolved result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First linguistic mention; exactly one child visible to both participants. | Does overt shared anchoring require further discourse prominence beyond visible uniqueness? | Situational shared uses and bare definite readings exist. Their exact distribution in this context is unresolved; uniqueness alone does not force the suffix. |
| The same child was introduced previously by an indefinite; no competing child. | How does bare continuation compare with explicit shared retrieval? | Bare anaphora remains possible; a categorical second-mention suffix requirement would contradict the current description. No ranking of these clauses is supplied. |
| Two children equally visible and salient, with no gesture or distinguishing description. | Can either nominal resolve the intended child without further information? | The anchor encodes no choice between equally recoverable candidates. Contextual ambiguity must not be reported as ungrammatical morphology. |
| Shared knowledge identifies one particular child, without previous mention in this conversation. | Can a shared index be recovered through common-ground knowledge? | Prior linguistic mention is not the definition of shared access. Accommodation conditions require separate specification. |
| Speaker selects a child whom the addressee can independently identify. | Does `si` remain compatible with successful hearer identification? | The current account withholds a shared-retrieval requirement; it does not require hearer ignorance. |

An introduction with `si byl` followed by shared or bare reference is a possible analytical discourse progression, rather than an obligatory first-mention/second-mention paradigm. Record subsequent recoverability independently of the initial anchor.

## Demonstrative controls

Chapter 5, `sec:nm-def-double`, establishes `tak byl` (DEM.PROX child.DIR, ‘this child’) and `tak bylo` (DEM.PROX child.DIR.DEF, ‘this very child; this particular child here’). Use these established nominal fragments as the target expressions; any insertion into a further clause must be labelled a constructed frame.

| Matched context | Contrast to evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| One visible child; first mention; neutral pointing. | Is the additional shared condition licensed without an emphatic or contrastive presentation? |
| Two visible children; an identical pointing gesture identifies one. | Does double marking add a conventional restriction beyond the gesture, or an available discourse interpretation? |
| One previously mentioned child; same gesture and visual setting. | Compare anaphoric retrieval with the neutral deictic expression. |
| The child returns to discussion after another topic. | Test reintroduction without assuming that the suffix itself means topic shift. |

Record felicity, obligatory meaning, and optional emphatic/resumptive/contrastive effects separately. Co-occurrence alone does not establish redundant meaning. The grammar supplies no established interpretation for `si` plus an adnominal demonstrative.

## Kind, generic, and mass controls

Chapter 14, `ex:tc-generic-topic`, establishes `Vlak drže na hlučna` (‘The wolf howls at night’) as a generic kind-topic construction. This verifies a bare kind-denoting nominal in that construction. It does not independently show how overt anchors behave with discourse-anaphoric kinds.

| Interpretation held distinct | Controlled enquiry |
| --- | --- |
| One episodically relevant wolf versus the wolf kind. | Keep the noun constant but specify the intended ontological referent before comparing anchors; a changed predicate is an additional variable. |
| A kind mentioned earlier versus a previously mentioned individual wolf. | Does shared retrieval extend to the kind itself? The answer remains open. |
| Generic predication versus reference to an individuated kind in a non-generic predication. | Do not infer that an anchor's generic preference settles all kind reference. |
| Institutional activity versus ordinary location in one establishment. | Iridian institutional enrichment is unresolved. Schwarz's weak-article definites and English weak institutional definites are distinct diagnostics. |

The Class VIII direct-case contrasts are established in Chapter 4, `sec:nm-classes-vii-ix`, `sec:nm-num`, and `sec:nm-def-allomorph`:

| Unmarked | Shared-anchored | Constant morphology and independent interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pivo — beer.DIR.SG | pivó — beer.DIR.SG.DEF | Mass singular; an anchored quantity/variety/kind reading must be evaluated separately. |
| piva — beer.DIR.PL | pivá — beer.DIR.PL.DEF | Countable servings, varieties, or instances; plurality does not itself encode shared retrieval. |

The full direct-case predicate frame `Kroumaště na pivo so jeca` (‘There is still some beer left in the refrigerator’) is already established. Substituting the other forms into it creates diagnostic frames, not established contextual judgements. For plural substitutions, confirm the affirmative predicate's number compatibility independently; do not transfer them to `zada`, whose pivot restrictions are different. Bare `piva` is also transitive singular elsewhere in the paradigm, so isolated forms must carry their direct-plural gloss here.

Vary only the intended beer referent within each number pair: an unspecified mass, a previously delimited quantity, a named variety, or previously introduced servings. Determine whether that referent satisfies the noun's interpretation before testing shared accessibility. Anchored morphology alone does not select a portion, and the atomic apple-counting schema does not derive mass measurement.

## Relational nouns and bridging

Chapter 5, `ex:nm-implicit-poss`, supplies the following complete contrasts with the same predicate:

| Established clause | Gloss | Independent control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Čás konca. | head.DIR hurt-CONT | Possessor resolved from utterance context. |
| Že čás konca. | 1SG.IND head.DIR hurt-CONT | Overt speaker possessor; clarification or contrast. |
| Marcí čás konca. | Marek.IND head.DIR hurt-CONT | Overt nominal possessor. |

Hold the hurting head and situation constant while changing spontaneous self-report to a report about Marek, or introduce two equally salient potential possessors. Record possessor resolution separately from identification of the resulting head. An explicit possessor fixes ownership without overtly shared-anchoring the head; bare speaker-oriented `čás` supplies no evidence for covert `si`. Compare fixed versus covarying possessors only in independently licensed binding environments. The alternatives `pro_POSS` and contextual relational saturation remain open.

For bridging, use the English prompts ‘a house … its door’ and ‘a book … its author’ with explicit uniqueness controls: one versus multiple relevant doors, one versus multiple authors, and presence versus absence of previous mention of the dependent referent. These prompts add no Iridian lexical forms or judgements. Establishing an antecedent's index is separate from resolving a relational argument and retrieving the related individual. German part–whole/producer article preferences do not determine Iridian marking.

## Scope and covariation

Retain the same lexical nominal while comparing affirmative existence, negation, belief, desire, conditionals, and universal dependencies. New clauses must be formed from independently verified case, alignment, aspect, mood, and embedding rules before they can serve as Iridian examples. Record separately (a) speaker identification, (b) shared accessibility, (c) asserted existential commitment, and (d) fixed versus varying reference across the operator's domain.

The English schema ‘Every child who bought a book read the book’ tests whether the book varies with the quantified child–book cases. A constant `g(i)` cannot derive that dependency; varying assignments or situations require an independently specified binder/update mechanism. The current account does not establish those Iridian readings. Likewise, `si hroná lobek` is not licensed by the surface template: selection of a three-apple plurality differs from selection of one apple before cardinal composition. Universal quantification over a singleton-restricted property does not derive quantification over every member of a salient group.
