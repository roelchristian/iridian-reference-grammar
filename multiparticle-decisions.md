# Multiparticle decisions: working dossier

This dossier records descriptive gaps and proposed diagnostics; it does not
license new Iridian combinations. Current `grammar.tex` and `chapters/` are
normative. Every order listed as a candidate below is **unjudged**, and English
paraphrases identify candidate interpretations rather than Iridian translations.
The expanded chapter prose describes the limits of the current rules; adopting
an option in this dossier would be a separate language-specification decision.

## Baseline and status distinctions

- Established zone precedence: upper periphery > high topic > `by` >
  Comm-field > phasal particle > predicate. This orders independently licensed
  positions, without licensing every possible occupant or combination.
- `vy` and `so` exclude one another in the single phasal position.
- Established combinations include `och vy/so`, `mó vy`, `dlí so`,
  `jas … och`, `jas … vy/so`, and `jas … by … vy/so`.
- `kamo` with `vy/so` has two specifically bounded compositions, with
  fixed focus alternatives. This does not establish general scope permutation.
- Repetition, same-field stacking, cross-field productivity and a general
  maximum number of particles remain unspecified.
- Keep four judgements separate: structural exclusion; unavailable reading;
  unsatisfied semantic/discourse background; and unspecified distribution.
  A possible interpretation is not evidence that its expression is licensed.

Principal anchors are `sec:particle-combinations` in Chapter 5,
`sec:th-particle-framework` and `sec:th-exclusive-phasal` in Chapter 6,
`sec:complex-evidential` in Chapter 8, and `sec:disc-final-particles` in Chapter 9.

`kamo` is the sole exclusive–scalar form. All pairs below remain unspecified
for co-occurrence and relative order; neither their listing nor their semantic
compatibility is a positive judgement. For each pair, record both candidate
orders separately before assigning interpretations.

| Field | Pair families |

individual particle require separate decisions; the distinct-pair inventory

## Rule options and a conservative route

| Option | Consequence requiring a decision |
|---|---|
| Exclusive occupation | A field or particular position admits one occupant; this would prohibit presently unresolved pairs. |
| Ordered subpositions | Distinct positions license particular combinations and precedence; the additional hierarchy needs independent motivation. |
| Restricted adjunction or recursion | Multiple operators are structurally possible, subject to independently stated licensing, type and ordering restrictions. |
| Construction-specific inventory | Particular pairings and interpretations are licensed individually; larger sequences do not follow automatically. |
| Bounded productivity | An explicit rule licenses a defined class of stacks, with specified exclusions, domains and composition. |

Retain the present partial zone ordering and construction-specific established
rules while investigating the gaps. Begin with pair records, then consider a
productive rule only where it captures several independently specified cases.
Do not give each particle a new head merely to reproduce a desired order;
equally, do not infer a single-occupant restriction from a shared field label.
Tagalog second-position displacement does not motivate such displacement in
Iridian, whose ordinary particle placement has a different description.

## Common diagnostic protocol

Each record needs: co-occurrence status; candidate surface orders; allowed
interveners and host; semantic inputs; interpreted nesting; focus associate and
alternative sets; at-issue and background contributions; perspective holder;
source; evaluation time; clause domain; clause type; and prosodic/register limits.

Start with one finite affirmative declarative and a constant proposition.
Separate the reference time from any earlier stage and the utterance time.
Fix the relevant people/actions, including the actual associate, before varying
one contextual condition. Use positive and negative contexts for each lexical
contribution independently, then contexts separating candidate compositions.
Afterwards vary negation, question/directive force and embedding individually.
Positive and negative controls below supply or remove candidate conditions;
they are not predictions of Iridian acceptability.

For an attitude, distinguish its **holder**, its **object** and its **source**:
reporting someone else's inference is different from making an inference from
their report. For questions, distinguish a possible answer, the question
denotation and the act of asking. For focus particles, record each alternative
set independently instead of assuming that two particles share one set.

Truth-equivalent or commuting operations need discourse diagnostics; different
notation alone does not establish different readings. Projected presuppositions
can also make rival scopes indistinguishable. Record entailment, equivalence
and undefinedness explicitly; do not manufacture two-way contextual contrasts.

## Priority diagnostic records

### 1. Addition and exclusivity: `och` with `kamo`

Candidate orders, both unjudged: `och kamo`; `kamo och`.
Compare ADD(EXCL(P)) and EXCL(ADD(P)), specifying both alternative sets first.
Begin with people {Janek, Marek, Matuš} and activities {sleeping, studying},
evaluated at one time; choose Janek/studying as the actual associate.

- Positive background: only Janek sleeps, and only Janek studies. This may
  support both an added exclusive proposition and an exclusive additive claim;
  acceptance here would not distinguish them.
- Potential separator: Janek and Marek sleep, but only Janek studies. Addition
  of the complete proposition “only Janek studies” lacks the corresponding
  “only Janek sleeps” background, whereas Janek individually has the sleeping
  background for “Janek also studies”. Determine projection for excluded
  alternatives before treating the latter as a well-defined EXCL(ADD(P)) case.
- Controls: remove every salient additive alternative while retaining exclusive
  studying; then allow Marek to study while retaining the additive background.

Decision: same versus distinct associates; background projection; scope options;
and whether any pronunciation order expresses the contrast. English “also only”
does not settle any of these Iridian properties.

### 2. Interim priority and exclusivity: `dlí` with `kamo`

Candidate orders, both unjudged: `dlí kamo`; `kamo dlí`.
Compare a provisional exclusive arrangement with exclusivity over provisional
courses of action. Fix people {Janek, Marek, Matuš}, sleeping as the action,
and a plan with an immediate stage and a later decision stage.

- Positive context: only Janek is assigned to sleep during the immediate stage;
  the allocation after the later decision is open.
- Separator to evaluate: Janek is assigned to sleep temporarily, while Marek
  sleeps throughout the whole plan. “Only Janek has sleeping as an interim
  course” can differ from “for now, only Janek sleeps”. These are proposed
  targets, not definitions already supplied by `dlí`.
- Controls: remove the later open stage but preserve exclusivity; then make
  Janek and Marek equally provisional sleepers while preserving the plan.

Decision: does `dlí` target a proposition, an instruction, or a structured plan,
and can exclusivity compare those objects? Recheck with action alternatives
only after the person-alternative case has a specified interpretation.

### 3. Addition and affirmation: `och` with `mó`

Candidate orders, both unjudged: `och mó`; `mó och`.
Compare ratifying an additive proposition with adding a ratified proposition.
Fix Janek's arrival, Marek as the salient other arrival, and one speaker/time.

- Positive context: Marek's arrival is established; Janek's arrival was doubted
  and is now verified. Both addition and ratification have support.
- Target contrast: the dispute concerns whether Janek arrived at all versus
  whether his arrival counts as another arrival in the relevant series.
- Controls: no other arrival is relevant, although Janek's arrival was doubted;
  conversely, another arrival is relevant but no ratification target is salient.

Decision: what does ratification contribute beyond the proposition's truth,
and can addition range over ratification acts? If the two compositions have
identical truth and background conditions, record one reading with alternative
analyses until a discourse or embedding contrast differentiates them.

### 4. Mirativity and attribution: `oče` with `či`

Candidate orders, both unjudged: `oče či`; `či oče`.
Fix P = Janek has arrived. Distinguish speaker S, reporter R, reporting time
and S's current realisation time; keep P and its phasal background constant.

- Target A: R neutrally reports P; S unexpectedly realises P on hearing it.
- Target B: R reports their own earlier surprise at P; S already expects P
  and merely relays R's report. This distinguishes the holder of mirativity.
- Target C: S is surprised that R reported P while remaining doubtful of P;
  this tests an attitude towards the report event rather than its content.
- Controls: direct discovery with no attributed source; then an attributed
  proposition with neither surprise nor newly salient realisation for S or R.

Decision: permissible holders, objects and times; whether reportative stance
can contain another attitude; and whether any candidate order differentiates
these targets. Compatibility of surprise and hearsay does not license a pair.

### 5. Inference and speculation: `hladě` with `kady`

Candidate orders, both unjudged: `hladě kady`; `kady hladě`.
Fix P = Marek is a doctor, one evidence set, and one assessment time initially.

- Same-holder context: S has suggestive evidence for P but openly entertains
  alternatives. Determine whether this supports both contributions, is merely
  one inferential stance, or produces an incompatible commitment combination.
- Nested target A: S infers from R's behaviour that R is speculating about P.
- Nested target B: S speculates whether R's evidence supports an inference to P.
  Keep S/R distinct; neither target is automatically a reading of a bare stack.
- Controls: direct verified knowledge rather than inference; then unsupported
  guessing with no evidential basis. Repeat with distinct earlier/current times
  only after the simultaneous same-holder case has been evaluated.

Decision: commitment strength versus semantic type, attitude attribution and
whether a clausal complement is required to express the nested targets.

### 6. Speech-act families: `jas`, `nadě`, `mlada`

Candidate orders, all unjudged: `jas nadě` / `nadě jas`;
`jas mlada` / `mlada jas`; `nadě mlada` / `mlada nadě`.

- `jas–nadě`: after discussing Marek, the speaker turns to Janek and draws
  a conclusion about Janek from his absent coat. Supply both a discourse
  reorientation and grounds for the conclusion. Remove each independently.
  Compare reorienting to a conclusion with drawing a conclusion about a
  changed discourse course; the latter may require explicit content instead.
- `jas–mlada`: shift from an actual plan for Marek to a wish about Janek.
  Contrast a discourse shift introducing a wish with a wish that a contrasting
  state should obtain. Controls remove the shift or the non-actual wish.
- `nadě–mlada`: circumstances lead S to formulate a wish; contrast this with
  an imagined world within which a conclusion follows. Hold the wish-holder
  fixed and state whether the inferential grounds belong to actuality or that
  imagined world. Controls remove the grounds or assert the wished-for event
  as established actuality rather than a desired possibility.

Decision: the target of each discourse relation, actual versus hypothetical
evaluation, and whether two framing acts are jointly realised in one clause.
These contexts do not decide between recursion and distinct subpositions.

## Cross-field, domain and final-particle decisions

- Catalogue SpeechAct–Evid combinations and upper–Comm/phasal combinations
  separately; zone precedence is insufficient to license them. Preserve known
  constructions and mark every additional pair or triple unjudged.
- For questions, distinguish surprise at P, surprise at asking ?P, reporting
  another person's ?P, and asking for a report-supported answer. Control overt
  `by` versus independently permitted null question marking without equating
  its absence with absence of interrogative force.
- For embedding, locate each particle in its matrix or embedded domain before
  identifying source/holder. Compare S reporting R's inference with S inferring
  from R's report; include inner `-al/-l` and `po` only under their own rules.
- For final particles, investigate all six distinct pairs among `len`, `prav`,
  `však`, `blěc`, their repetition and their interaction with the preverbal
  series. Prioritise `mó … prav`, `jas … však` and stance … `len`: reinforcement,
  separate targets and redundancy are competing possibilities, not judgements.
- For longer stacks, fix binary brackets and evaluate every contribution's
  background. A pairwise-licensed network does not prove triple productivity;
  a length limit, if adopted, needs an explicit rule rather than intuition.

## Research anchors and limits

- Hsieh & Erlewine (2024), [LSA handout](https://mitcho.com/research/handout-lsa2024.pdf),
  §§3–5 and appendix A: independent order/scope diagnostics; projected
  presuppositions can obscure the relative scope of `na rin`.
- Hsieh & Erlewine (2025), [TripleA contribution](https://mitcho.com/research/clitics-triplea.html),
  §§4–5: construction-specific `pa lang` and `na lang`; these do not supply
  Iridian licensing. Their [handbook page](https://mitcho.com/research/clitics-handbook.html)
  still identifies the clitics chapter as a draft, advocating syntactically
  constrained postsyntactic placement for Tagalog.
- Tan Almazán (2019), [dissertation](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/10486/692396/1/tan_almazan_jennifer.pdf),
  §3.3.2.2, pp. 127–129: `daw yata` order/holder contrasts and fixed `daw kayâ`
  with different interpretations; §4.3.4: interrogative scope. These findings
  motivate separate source and holder records, without transferring readings.
- AnderBois (2023), [author manuscript on `pala`](https://anderbois.github.io/PDFs/AnderBois_Tagalog_pala.pdf),
  §5.2 and §6.3: question updates and `nga pala` remembering; attitude objects
  and construction-specific restrictions cannot be reduced to simple precedence.
- Fang & Hengeveld (2022), [Mandarin](https://doi.org/10.1111/stul.12198), §5.5:
  semantic categories and scope layers differ. Their Functional Discourse
  Grammar layers are not the Minimalist projections adopted for Iridian.
- Lohmann & Koops (2026), [German combinations](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1470542726100245),
  §§4–5.4: co-occurrence association, order and conventionalisation need separate
  treatment. Corpus preferences do not assign Iridian judgements.

## Adoption and cross-chapter checklist

Editorial work can clarify existing status distinctions, inventory the gaps,
verify comparisons and improve cross-references. New co-occurrence judgements,
scope readings, holders, productive rules and projections are design choices.

Before adoption: record an explicit decision and its domain; check positive and
negative controls; trace at-issue and background content; identify indistinguishable
analyses; verify morphology and glosses if constructing Iridian examples; and
check that the decision does not generalise the bounded exclusive–phasal rule.

Update Chapter 5's descriptions and status table with Chapter 6's composition
and question analysis; update Chapter 8's attribution/domain treatment and
Chapter 9's final-particle interface where relevant. Revise Chapter 1's spine
and diagrams last, preserving a distinction between established distribution
and analytical structure. Run the repository's sequential build, diagnostic,
publication and diff checks, then inspect affected PDF pages.
