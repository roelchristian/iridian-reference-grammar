\chapter{Information Structure}\label{ch:infstr}
\label{sec:topic-comment}% cross-reference compatibility

Iridian distinguishes the participant selected by its alignment system from
 the referent about whom an utterance makes a predication. Although the
 direct-case argument commonly serves both functions, a transitive-case agent
 may be topicalised while the patient retains direct case, and an existential
 pivot need not be an aboutness topic at all. These distributions motivate a
 topic-oriented analysis in which case, nominal anchoring, and information
 structure contribute independently to the organisation of the clause.

\section{The topic: conceptual and formal foundations}\label{sec:tc-foundations}
\subsection{Aboutness and the logical subject of predication}
\label{sec:tc-logical-subject}

The \term{aboutness topic} identifies the individual, kind, or subject matter
 under which the following assertion is organised. The \term{comment} supplies
 the predication made about that topic. This discourse relation differs from
 both the grammatical privilege of the \term{absolutive pivot}, which bears
 direct case, and the semantic relation between a logical subject and a
 predicate. A proposition may be represented as $P(a)$ without establishing
 $a$ as the current topic of the conversation. Gécseg's discussion of Hungarian
 makes this distinction explicit: logical predication and pragmatic aboutness
 are separate levels of analysis \cite{gecseg2006}.

In an imperfective active clause, the direct-case pivot is normally the agent
 or sole participant; in a perfective transitive with \ird{-n-}, it is the
 patient. The following pair illustrates this aspect-conditioned alternation:

\pex\label{ex:tc-topic-subj}
\a
\begingl
\gla Marek tak tomem kupujime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Ind{} buy-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is buying this book.}
      \lingcontext{nominative domain; agent = absolutive pivot; Marek-topic context}//
\endgl
\a
\begingl
\gla Tak tóm Marku koupnik.//
\glb \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Dir{} Marek.\Trs{} buy-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{This book, Marek bought.}
      \lingcontext{ergative domain; patient = absolutive pivot; book-topic context}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent Marek remains the buyer in both clauses, while the book changes
 from indirect to direct case. In a conversation about Marek, the first clause
 may describe his current activity; in a conversation about the book, the
 second may explain what happened to it. Those contexts support the indicated
 topic interpretations, but the aspectual contrast by itself establishes only
 the case alternation. A completed event can equally form part of a continuing
 discussion of Marek (§\,\ref{sec:tc-marked-topics}).

For a referential topic $a$ and a comment denoting a property $P$, the assertion
 ordinarily contributes $P(a)$. Establishing $a$ as the subject matter concerns
 the organisation of that contribution in discourse. This distinction follows
 Krifka's separation of common-ground content from common-ground management
 \cite[§\,2.2]{krifka2008}; it does not entail that every information-structural
 contrast is truth-conditionally inert.

\subsection{Absolutive pivot, alignment, and default topicality}
\label{sec:tc-abs-align}

The direct-case pivot precedes the remaining arguments in the neutral orders
 illustrated below. When the discourse supplies no competing topic, a
 referential pivot is also the default candidate for aboutness:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em, labelwidth=!, labelindent=0em]
\item[\upshape Absolutive--Topic Alignment:] A referential direct-case pivot
 is preferentially interpreted as the aboutness topic in an unmarked finite
 clause, provided that the discourse supports a topic--comment articulation.
\end{description}

\noindent This preference concerns interpretation rather than case licensing.
 Asp.Align and Voice determine the case frame within the lower clause;
 \synhead{TopP} represents an independently established aboutness relation.
 A pivot can remain below that projection, whether it is discourse-given,
 quantificational, or part of a topicless presentation.

A simple applicative assigns direct case to its applied participant, whose
benefactive, recipient, or other licensed role is established by the verbal
construction (§\,\ref{sec:vm-appl}). This grammatical selection makes the
participant accessible to the corresponding participant nominal and relative,
without assigning aboutness topichood. A causative or directive configuration
can select another pivot while retaining the applied argument in the clause;
the discourse topic is then determined independently, as in ordinary
transitives. The controlled sequences in §\,\ref{sec:disc-valency-continuity}
illustrate patient, agent, and beneficiary continuity with overt reference.

\pex\label{ex:tc-aspect-topic}
\a
\begingl
\gla Marek travě piaštíme.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{} bread.\Ind{} eat-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is eating bread.}
      \lingcontext{nominative domain; agent = direct; Marek-topic context}//
\endgl
\a
\begingl
\gla Travó Marku piaštník.//
\glb bread.\Dir{}.\Def{} Marek.\Trs{} eat-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek ate the bread.}
      \lingcontext{ergative domain; patient = direct; bread-topic context}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent The progressive presents eating in progress, whereas the perfective
 presents a bounded event. Marek and the bread are suitable topics in the
 respective contexts, but neither the progressive nor the perfective requires
 that topic choice. The topic readings therefore depend on how each clause
 contributes to the current discussion, within the preference for a
 referential pivot established above.

\subsection{The formal properties of ordinary topics}
\label{sec:tc-ordinary-topics}

Ordinary topics supply a discourse address for the comment. They include
 hearer-accessible individuals, recoverable null arguments, kinds, and
 individuated referents being introduced for discussion. Prior mention and
 suffixal anchoring can help establish a topic in context, without being
 obligatory conditions on every topic.

\pex\label{ex:tc-topic-types}
\a\label{ex:tc-anch-topic}%
\begingl
\gla Marko vy zdrávime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is already sleeping.} \lingcontext{anchored referential topic}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-null-topic}%
\begingl
\gla By vy zdrávime?//
\glb \textsc{q} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Is he already sleeping?} \lingcontext{null continuing topic; referent established}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-generic-topic}%
\begingl
\gla Vlak drže na hlučna.//
\glb wolf.\Dir{} night in howl-\Cont{}//
\glft \trsl{The wolf howls at night.} \lingcontext{generic kind-denoting topic; bare singular}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-spec-indef-topic}%
\begingl
\gla Si byl zdrávime.//
\glb \Sanch{} child.\Dir{} sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{A certain child is sleeping.}
      \lingcontext{new speaker-anchored topic; speaker has a particular child in mind}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent The suffix in \ird{Marko} explicitly anchors Marek within the shared
 discourse, whereas the null expression in (\ref{ex:tc-null-topic}) continues
 an already recoverable referent. The generic wolf names a kind. In
 (\ref{ex:tc-spec-indef-topic}), \ird{si} presents a particular child selected
 by the speaker, whom the utterance introduces as its subject matter under
 an introduction such as `Let me tell you about a child I know'. New
 indefinites can establish topics without being discourse-given
 \cite[§\,5.1]{krifka2008}; in Iridian, the aboutness relation belongs to
 the clause rather than to the speaker-anchoring proclitic
 (§\,\ref{sec:nm-spec}). The same \ird{si byl} can therefore occur within
 a presentational answer to `What is there in the house?', where the child's
 presence is asserted without making that child the subject matter of a
 separate comment
 (§\,\ref{sec:tc-topicless}).

\subsection{Quantificational pivots and negative concord}
\label{sec:tc-quantificational-pivots}

\ird{Deno že zastráda} \trsl{Nothing is lacking to me}
 (\ref{ex:nm-negative-concord}) begins with the direct-case theme of
 \ird{strád-} \trsl{be lacking}; \ird{že} is the indirect-case experiencer.
 The initial negative indefinite is existentially interpreted within the scope
 of negation, so that the clause denies the existence of a relevant thing
 lacking to the speaker. Its position does not establish a referential
 aboutness topic.

The negative indefinite undergoes ordinary placement in \synhead{OrdP},
 below any independently licensed topic, while its lower chain occurrence
 retains existential interpretation under the negative operator. Negative
 concord is licensed by that operator and its Agree relation with the
 indefinite; neither licensing nor narrow scope requires a topical feature
 (§§\,\ref{sec:vm-neg}, \ref{sec:nm-negative-concord}). This dependency
 preserves the construction's narrow interpretation without requiring every
 nominal chain to reconstruct for every interpretive purpose.

\subsection{Topic position in the clause spine}
\label{sec:tc-clause-spine}

A clause-internal aboutness topic occupies Spec,\synhead{TopP} in the
 analysis adopted here. The principal left-peripheral hierarchy is:

\begin{center}
\synhead{TopP} $>$ \synhead{FocP} $>$ \synhead{OrdP} $>$
\synhead{SpeechActP} $>$ \synhead{EvidP}$_{\mathrm{outer}}$ $>$
\synhead{PolP} $>$ \synhead{IP}
\end{center}

\noindent \synhead{TopP} is optional: lower arguments retain their case and
 remain interpretable when no aboutness dependency is projected. The comment
 of a topicalised clause comprises the domain below \synhead{TopP}, including
 clause typing and focus. Ordinary placement precedes outer evidential and speech-act material,
 while external frames attach outside the clause-internal topic
 dependency (§\,\ref{sec:tc-multiple-topics}). Here \synhead{IP} includes
 Mood and the inner verbal evidential, which remain below focus
 (Table~\ref{tab:th-clause-spine}).

The interrogative particle \ird{by} realises locally head-initial
 Pol\textsuperscript{0}. Ordinary arguments and modifiers move to the
 finite placement edge in \synhead{OrdP}, independently of their topic
 interpretation, and therefore precede \ird{by} and the stance particles.
 Its ordered placement requirements select the pivot first, then the remaining
 eligible constituents in lower order, with later movements tucked beneath
 earlier edge occupants (§\,\ref{sec:pivot-placement}). Lower occurrences
 preserve case, binding and operator interpretation. Consequently,
 pre-\ird{by} pronunciation alone does not diagnose Spec,\synhead{TopP}.

Linked topicalisation is analysed as an A$'$-dependency between the high
 topic and a lower argument position. Case is licensed independently in the
 verbal and aspectual domain, so movement to the topic field preserves the
 argument's case. The case-preserving dependency distinguishes linked topics from resumptive
 hanging topics (§\,\ref{sec:tc-multiple-topics}).

This high A$'$-dependency is distinct from ordinary finite placement,
 which does not assign an aboutness feature or uniformly constitute A-movement. In particular, a direct-case
 patient does not exclude linked topicalisation of a transitive-case agent
 (§\,\ref{sec:tc-marked-topics}). A formal search for an aboutness-bearing
 argument must distinguish that property from the lower configuration that
 selects the direct participant. Merely encountering a direct-case nominal
 is therefore not sufficient to diagnose intervention in the topic dependency;
 its eligibility for that dependency matters as well. The permitted agent
 dependency still has to respect the locality of the construction, rather
 than acquiring unrestricted access through the case distinction.

The same separation applies to interrogative dependencies. Ordinary
 wh-placement precedes the particle field without a dedicated wh-fronting
 dependency; its lower occurrence supplies the variable interpreted under Pol.
 Marked wh-fronting additionally targets Spec,\synhead{FocP}, above the
 ordinary placement field (§\,\ref{sec:th-wh-interrogation}). The retained
 paradigms establish focus $>$ ordinary constituents, and linked topic $>$
 ordinary constituents, without independently licensing every combination of
 overt topic and marked focus. Construction-specific accessibility is
 summarised in Table~\ref{tab:construction-accessibility}.

\section{Topic and the information-structural field}\label{sec:tc-isf}
\subsection{Topic versus focus}\label{sec:tc-focus-contrast}

A topic identifies what the utterance is about, whereas focus relates its
 contribution to relevant alternatives, including the alternatives supplied by
 a question under discussion \cite[§§\,3.1, 3.4]{krifka2008}. In an answer to
 `What is Marek reading?', the book expression may carry narrow information
 focus while Marek remains the topic. In an answer to `What is Marek doing?',
 the larger reading predication may be focused. Ordinary information focus
 does not itself entail exhaustive identification.

\pex\label{ex:tc-top-foc}
\a\label{ex:tc-top-only}%
\begingl
\gla Marek tak tomem obrójime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Ind{} read-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is reading this book.}
      \lingcontext{Marek-topic context; ordinary placement within the comment}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-top-plus-foc}%
\begingl
\gla Marko tak tomem obrójime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Ind{} read-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is reading this book.}
      \lingcontext{anchored Marek; compatible with narrow focus on the book}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent These strings differ in the anchoring of Marek, while the book
 expression occupies the same position relative to the other overt words.
 They therefore illustrate the compatibility of anchoring with different
 contextual focus assignments, rather than an observable movement contrast.
 \synhead{FocP} represents marked displacement in the clause-spine analysis;
 the present examples illustrate anchoring and contextual focus rather than
 an independently diagnosed movement dependency.

In nominative-domain clauses, low manner adverbs normally precede
 non-pivot objects (§\,\ref{sec:word-order}).
 Object placement relative to such adverbs can serve as a positional
 diagnostic when its focus interpretation is independently established.
 The Turkish analysis of Özkan Grigoraş distinguishes higher discourse
 positions from lower given material and in-situ information focus
 \cite[§\,4.4.2]{ozkangrigoras2020}; it offers a comparative model for this separation.

\subsection{Topic versus sentence adverbials and peripheral particles}
\label{sec:tc-adverbials}

Temporal and locative frames restrict the situation in which the proposition
 is evaluated, while an aboutness topic identifies its subject matter.
 Evidential and clause-typing particles contribute further operators. Their
 initial position does not make them arguments or aboutness topics:

\pex\label{ex:tc-adv-contrast}
\a\label{ex:tc-adv-true-top}%
\begingl
\gla Marek by vy zdrávime?//
\glb Marek.\Dir{} \textsc{q} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Is Marek sleeping yet?}
      \lingcontext{true topic; \ird{Marek} in Spec,\synhead{TopP}}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-adv-adv}%
\begingl
\gla Bych Marek by vy zdrávime?//
\glb yesterday Marek.\Dir{} \textsc{q} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Was Marek already asleep yesterday?}
      \lingcontext{temporal frame; \ird{Marek} undergoes ordinary placement}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-adv-evid}%
\begingl
\gla Marko oče vy zdrávime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \textsc{mir} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Surprisingly, Marek is already sleeping.}
      \lingcontext{evidential \ird{oče} follows the topic \ird{Marko}}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent In (\ref{ex:tc-adv-true-top}), Marek has the explicitly assigned
 topic interpretation. In (\ref{ex:tc-adv-adv}), the temporal frame precedes
 Marek, whose ordinary placement puts him before \ird{by} without requiring
 an aboutness dependency. In (\ref{ex:tc-adv-evid}), the anchored topic
 precedes mirative \ird{oče}. Case, contextual aboutness and the independently
 established frame distinguish these relations, whereas their common
 pre-particle pronunciation does not.

\section{Topic in discourse: drop, absence, and multiplicity}\label{sec:tc-discourse}
\subsection{Linked topics, hanging topics, and frames}
\label{sec:tc-multiple-topics}

A linked argument topic retains the case associated with its lower position.
 A hanging topic instead establishes a discourse referent outside the argument
 dependency, whose relation to the comment may be expressed by a resumptive
 form. The following constructions illustrate the latter organisation:

\pex\label{ex:tc-frame-top}
\a\label{ex:tc-frame-town}%
\begingl
\gla Tak dvor Marko ten na býdlime.//
\glb \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} town.\Dir{} Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Ind{} in live-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{As for this town, Marek is living in it.}
      \lingcontext{hanging frame + clause topic; resumptive \ird{ten} co-refers with frame topic}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-frame-marek}%
\begingl
\gla Marko tak tóm bych ktářnik.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Dir{} yesterday read-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{As for Marek, this book he read yesterday.}
      \lingcontext{hanging topic + direct-case patient}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent In (\ref{ex:tc-frame-town}), direct-case \ird{tak dvor} establishes
 the town as a frame, while indirect-case \ird{ten} supplies the complement of
 \ird{na} within the clause. The external nominal therefore does not bear the
 case of the resumed locative. In (\ref{ex:tc-frame-marek}), direct-case
 \ird{Marko} is a hanging topic; a linked perfective agent would instead bear
 transitive \ird{Markuto} (§\,\ref{sec:tc-marked-topics}). The patient
 \ird{tak tóm} remains the direct-case pivot, and the unexpressed reader is
 recovered as Marek through the hanging-topic relation.

External frames may combine with a clause-internal topic, yielding distinct
 outer and inner relations to the comment. This distinction has an independent
 comparative precedent in Aissen's analysis of Mayan \cite{aissen1992}.
 In Iridian, the examples establish the coexistence of the two constructions
 without requiring unrestricted recursion of identical topic projections.

\subsection{Topic drop and null topics}\label{sec:tc-topic-drop}

A continuing topic may remain unexpressed when its referent is recoverable.
 The grammar represents this configuration with a silent topic dependency,
 while allowing other recoverable arguments to be null within the comment.
 Consequently, absence of a nominal does not by itself identify its syntactic
 position. An overt nominal or demonstrative can reintroduce a referent when
 a change of topic or competing antecedents would otherwise obscure reference.

\pex\label{ex:tc-topdrop}
\a\label{ex:tc-topdrop-overt}%
\begingl
\gla Marko domů chódime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} home go-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is going home.} \lingcontext{overt topic; referent introduced}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-topdrop-null1}%
\begingl
\gla Dumě na by vy jeca?//
\glb house.\Ind{} in \textsc{q} already \Exst{}-\Cont{}//
\glft \trsl{Is he home yet?}
      \lingcontext{null topic; referent continues from (\ref{ex:tc-topdrop-overt})}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-topdrop-null2}%
\begingl
\gla Domů so chódime.//
\glb home still go-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{He is still going home.}
      \lingcontext{null topic continues; the reply places him on the journey home}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent Marek remains the intended referent throughout this exchange.
 The locative phrase precedes the question particle through ordinary
 placement, and phasal \ird{vy} remains inside its comment. The answer continues the journey rather than placing
 Marek at home. This sequence illustrates recoverable topic continuity, with
 no competition between an established topic and a distinct grammatical pivot.

Iridian has no verbal person agreement to identify a null argument. Its
 interpretation depends on discourse accessibility, the established topic,
 the current predicate, and the case frame. The preceding direct-case pivot
 supplies the neutral antecedent preference where no stronger topic has been
 established; its case does not impose an exceptionless coreference rule.
 Connected examples and the limits of this preference are discussed in
 §\,\ref{sec:topic-continuity}.

\subsection{Apparent and genuinely topicless clauses}
\label{sec:tc-topicless}

A clause with an unexpressed continuing topic differs from a genuinely
 topicless presentation, which asserts a situation without selecting an
 individual as the subject matter of a further comment. The latter is
 analysed without an aboutness dependency in \synhead{TopP}; a direct-case
 argument retains its lower predicate-domain occurrence while ordinary
 placement supplies its pronounced position.

\pex\label{ex:tc-topicless}
\a\label{ex:tc-apparent-topicless}%
\begingl
\gla by koupnik?//
\glb \textsc{q} buy-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{Did he buy it?}
      \lingcontext{apparent topicless; null patient and buyer, both recoverable}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-pres-topicless}%
\begingl
\gla Si byl dumě na jeca.//
\glb \Sanch{} child.\Dir{} house.\Ind{} in \Exst{}-\Cont{}//
\glft \trsl{There is a certain child in the house.}
      \lingcontext{topicless presentation; speaker selects the child whose presence is asserted}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-exist-topicless}%
\begingl
\gla Kroumašťe na pivo so jeca.//
\glb refrigerator.\Ind{} in beer.\Dir{} still \Exst{}-\Cont{}//
\glft \trsl{There is still some beer left in the refrigerator.}
      \lingcontext{genuinely topicless existential; quantity remaining is asserted}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent In (\ref{ex:tc-apparent-topicless}), perfective \ird{koupnik}
 retains its patient-direct, agent-transitive case frame despite omission of
 both arguments. The discourse must identify which referent, if either,
 continues as the aboutness topic. The existential clauses in
 (\ref{ex:tc-pres-topicless}--\ref{ex:tc-exist-topicless}) have presentational
 readings: the child's presence and the remaining beer constitute the
 assertion. The same nominal can become a topic in subsequent discourse.
 Speaker selection by \ird{si} consequently does not force a categorical
 topic--comment reading, just as direct case does not force one. A bare
 \ird{byl} in the same existential frame would leave the speaker's ability
 to identify the child unmarked. After either introduction, a following
 \ird{Zdrávime} \trsl{[He or she] is sleeping} can predicate sleep of that
 child if the discourse now selects it as topic; the omission depends on
 this recoverability, not on whether the first mention carried \ird{si}.

A negative quantificational pivot likewise retains lower interpretation
 while undergoing ordinary placement without acquiring referential aboutness (§\,\ref{sec:tc-quantificational-pivots}).
 Its negative-concord licensing differs from existential presentation, even
 though neither construction requires a high referential topic.

\section{Non-default topics: marked, promoted, and contrastive}\label{sec:tc-nondefault}
\subsection{Linked topics in non-direct case}\label{sec:tc-marked-topics}

A perfective transitive may topicalise its agent while preserving the
 patient-direct, agent-transitive case frame. The following clauses differ
 in which participant the discourse selects for predication:

\pex\label{ex:tc-marked-top}
\a\label{ex:tc-neutral-top}%
\begingl
\gla Tak tóm Markuto koupnik.//
\glb \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Dir{} Marek.\Trs{}.\Def{} buy-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{This book, Marek bought.}
      \lingcontext{book-topic context; patient = direct-case pivot}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-marked-agent-top}%
\begingl
\gla Markuto tak tóm koupnik.//
\glb Marek.\Trs{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Dir{} buy-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{As for Marek, he bought this book.}
      \lingcontext{Marek-topic context; transitive agent in the high topic field}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent The first clause can answer a question about what happened to the
 book, whereas the second can continue a discussion of what Marek did.
 \ird{Markuto} retains transitive case and the book retains direct case;
 neither aspect nor case assignment changes with the aboutness relation.
 Both clauses retain shared anchoring on Marek and the same demonstrative
 on the book, so that the comparison holds aspect, case, and nominal anchoring
 constant while varying order and the contextual aboutness relation. A
 non-direct argument can therefore serve as topic without becoming the
 absolutive. The consequences for subsequent null reference require a
 separate comparison (§\,\ref{sec:disc-controlled-continuations}).

Agent topicalisation may express continuation, reintroduction, or shift,
 depending on context. Correction and contrast require their own discourse
 support; they are not entailed by transitive case, by the anchoring suffix,
 or by a topic interpretation. Under the linked-topic analysis, the agent
 occupies Spec,\synhead{TopP} in an A$'$-dependency with its lower position,
 while the direct-case book remains in the comment. The corresponding
 hanging-topic construction has unmarked \ird{Marko} instead
 (\ref{ex:tc-frame-marek}).

\subsection{Contrastive topic}\label{sec:tc-contrastive-topic}

A contrastive topic selects one member of a set about which parallel questions
 are under discussion. Under `What are Marek and Janek reading?', a clause
 about Marek can answer the subordinate question concerning him while leaving
 the question concerning Janek open. Under `Who is reading this book?', the
 name instead supplies a focus alternative. The contrastive topic contains a focus that evokes alternative
 topics and organises the exchange into subquestions, whereas the focus
 within the comment supplies alternatives relevant to the selected subquestion
 \cite[§§\,5.2, 6.2]{krifka2008}.

\pex\label{ex:tc-ctop}
\a\label{ex:tc-ctop-ordinary}%
\begingl
\gla Marko tak tomem ktářime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Ind{} read-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is reading this book.}
      \lingcontext{ordinary topic; no contrastive alternatives invoked}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-ctop-contr}%
\begingl
\gla Marko tak tomem ktářime, ale Janko tak tomem zaktářime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Ind{} read-\Prog{} but Janek.\Dir{}.\Def{} \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Ind{} \Neg{}-read-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is reading this book, but Janek is not reading this book.}
      \lingcontext{contrastive topic \ird{Marko}; Marek contrasted with Janek}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-ctop-obj}%
\begingl
\gla Tak tóm Marku koupnik, ale si list Marku zakoupnik.//
\glb \Dem{}.\Prox{}.\Dir{} book.\Dir{} Marek.\Trs{} buy-\Trs{}-\Pf{} but \Sanch{} letter.\Dir{} Marek.\Trs{} \Neg{}-buy-\Trs{}-\Pf{}//
\glft \trsl{This book, Marek bought; a certain letter, Marek did not buy.}
      \lingcontext{contrastive topic in ergative-domain clause; \ird{si} speaker-anchors the letter}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent The conjunction \ird{ale} makes the contrast between Marek and
 Janek, or between the book and the letter, overt. In a context organised into
 parallel questions about those referents, the initial nominals may function
 as contrastive topics. The conjunction alone does not diagnose that
 organisation: it also occurs in ordinary propositional contrasts.

Contrastive topics are described as having distinct phrasal prominence,
 without relocation of lexical stress (§\,\ref{sec:stress}). The pitch
 contour, boundary placement, and its distinction from narrow focus have no
 specified tonal analysis. A rising contour or an obligatory pause therefore
 cannot be inferred from the examples. The contrastive feature in
 \synhead{TopP} represents their discourse interpretation rather than a newly
 introduced particle or anchoring allomorph.

\section{The topic--anchoring interface}\label{sec:tc-anchoring-interface}

Shared anchoring, expressed by a suffixal allomorph or a class-specific
 cumulative ending, marks accessibility within the shared discourse, whereas
 \ird{si} supports speaker-anchored individuation
 (§\,\ref{sec:nm-spec}). Topicality concerns what is predicated about such
 a referent. An anchored argument can remain backgrounded inside the comment,
 and a new topic can be introduced without a hearer-shared anchor. The
 \term{Topicality--Anchoring Harmony} preference
 (§\,\ref{sec:nm-topic-interface}) describes their functional
 compatibility rather than a requirement that Top value nominal anchoring.

\pex\label{ex:tc-anch-contrast}
\a\label{ex:tc-anch-overt}%
\begingl
\gla Marko vy zdrávime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{}.\Def{} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is already sleeping.}
      \lingcontext{anchored topic; explicit discourse tracking}//
\endgl
\a\label{ex:tc-anch-bare}%
\begingl
\gla Marek vy zdrávime.//
\glb Marek.\Dir{} already sleep-\Prog{}//
\glft \trsl{Marek is already sleeping.}
      \lingcontext{unanchored topic; less explicit tracking}//
\endgl
\xe

\noindent Both clauses can assert that Marek is already sleeping while
 differing in explicit shared anchoring. Neither form alone determines
 whether Marek continues an old topic or introduces a new one. The same
 anchoring distinction is available in non-direct cases: an anchored
 transitive agent or indirect recipient need not be contrastively topical.
 In Distributed Morphology, Vocabulary Insertion realises the nominal features
 already present in the nominal domain, while the topic dependency is
 established independently in the clause. No additional topic exponent is
 required on the finite verb. The initial position of \ird{si} within its
 nominal pronunciation domain likewise follows the nominal PF rule
 (§\,\ref{sec:nm-si-linearisation}); it supplies no positional diagnostic
 for Spec,\synhead{TopP}. The same nominal anchoring contrast under a fixed
 aboutness interpretation is illustrated in
 §\,\ref{sec:disc-anchoring-controlled}.

\section{Descriptive generalisations and their limits}\label{sec:tc-summary}

Iridian's topic-oriented organisation rests on the coexistence of case-retaining
 linked topics, externally framed resumptive constructions, recoverable
 continuation, and topicless presentation. The direct-case pivot supplies a
 neutral aboutness candidate, whose selection remains sensitive to context.
 These interacting properties provide a more precise characterisation than
 an inference of topic prominence from alignment alone. Paul and Whitman's
 comparative survey shows that such commonly invoked diagnostics as null
 topics, multiple topics, overt marking, and wh-in-situ do not individually
 delimit a unique topic-prominent type \cite{paulwhitman2017}.

The examples distinguish high topics from lower pivots by their case
 relations, resumption, and independently specified discourse contexts.
 Position before \ird{by} is shared with ordinary non-topical placement. The examples motivate controlled contrasts involving focus displacement,
 competition between animate antecedents, extraction locality, and distinct
 topic contours, rather than demonstrating these distinctions independently. Contextually controlled comparisons are central to the
 documentation of topic, focus, and their contrastive counterparts
 \cite{aissen2026}. Since the Iridian examples are constructed illustrations
 of the grammar, their contextual interpretations are analytical assignments,
 rather than reports of elicited judgements or corpus frequencies.
