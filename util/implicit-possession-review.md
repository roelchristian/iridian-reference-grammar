# Implicit nominal possession: evidence and diagnostic limits

This review distinguishes comparative evidence from Iridian design specifications. It adds no external possessor, experiencer dative, applicative, agreement, declension class or new reflexive-binding rule. The adopted nominal account must distinguish permission to leave a semantic possessor unexpressed from the subsequent resolution of its reference. A continuing discourse topic can guide resolution; neither the latest grammatical pivot nor converb pivot continuity identifies an implicit possessor automatically.

## Provisional Iridian diagnostics

These are research questions, not recorded acceptability judgments. No positive or negative result is silently supplied.

1. **Covariation.** Contrast a fixed discourse possessor with the intended varying reading in the schema “every child says that their head hurts.” Determine whether each child's head can vary with the quantifier, controlling accessibility and perspective. A supported varying reading requires compositional variable interpretation, but would not alone distinguish a silent DP from semantic argument saturation.
2. **Reflexive locality.** Compare an implicit relational possessor with overt indirect reflexive **svy** in independently licensed local domains. Include a competing discourse centre and a nonlocal antecedent. The existing reflexive's restrictions remain authoritative; an omitted possessor is not automatically reflexive and receives no broader binding licence from frequent coreference.
3. **Alienable association.** Compare **dum** ‘house’ or **tóm** ‘book’ after an established ownership context, after mere spatial co-occurrence, and with ownership unknown. Separate contextual identification of an object from a genuine possessive interpretation. The noun's omission of an overt dependent does not itself demonstrate an obligatory covert possessor.
4. **Overt-reference controls.** Hold the predication constant while contrasting an implicit possessor with an ordinary overt indirect-case possessor. Test clarification, contrast and competing salient possessors without treating ambiguity or redundancy as categorical ungrammaticality. Overt reference and independently licensed local binding constrain discourse-based defaults.
5. **Anchoring and pivot controls.** Vary speaker anchoring **si**, shared anchoring, aboutness topic and grammatical pivot independently. Preserve the distinction between speaker-selected reference and speaker ownership. Same-pivot converb continuity alone predicts no possessor identity.

## Representation and scope

Relationality is a lexical-semantic dependency, independent of Classes I–IX. A head or kinship noun may supply a relational argument; an ordinary alienable need not. A provisional **pro_POSS** in the existing **PossP** is a useful notation for the possessive dependency, not a demonstrated silent DP. Direct contextual saturation remains an alternative. Neither analysis is established by the availability of an English possessive translation, and neither entails mechanical PF deletion of **že**.

The lexical and morphological shape of each Iridian example must be checked against current sources separately from this comparative review. The design introduces no first-person feature on a body-part noun, no possessor agreement on the predicate, and no additional clausal argument to obtain a contextual possessor reading. Generic relational uses require their own appropriate interpretation; they do not force a discourse individual into every nominal.
