# Stative paradigm and selection audit

These are constructed grammatical specifications, not native attestations. The independent literal matrix is `scripts/stative-fixtures.json`; `scripts/stative_diagnostics.py` checks selected phonological tokens and supplied semantic conditions. It is a bounded reference model, not a general analyser or a pronunciation generator.

## Adopted selection

- The seven short stems below have explicitly adopted finite Class A principal parts. Their simple progressive/PF selects a local G1 root; their retrospective/contemplative retains G0 with long ending. Finite class does not determine attributive class.
- Protected `tvírzn-` has Class C short aspect endings and lexical `í`. Its separately selected simple attributive `tvírzná` retains both long vowels.
- Become takes base-grade State, selecting `-él-` after an unprotected short stem and `-el-` with independently protected length. Its short outer endings are `-a/-ime/-ek/-av/-až`; Aspect does not reach through Become to regrade the property root.
- Free inchoative PF `-ek` has ordinary bound `-ik-` before participant `-ou` and relative `-ový`, and bound `-ice` before the direct linker. The stative perfective is independently high-vowel `-ik`.
- Negative indicative `za-` scopes above Asp, remains unstressed, and precedes every consonant-initial stem here. `vy` and `so` are external phasal particles. Neither supplies a covert morphological Become, although phase over a state can convey an onset as a discourse inference.
- The simple progressive is licensed only in an appropriate stage-level or expressive context. Simple PF requires a bounded-state context. A retrospective state does not entail entry into the state; contemplative is projective rather than a future tense.
- A continuous State relative is marked and needs explicit episodic, temporal or contrastive context. A simple property attributive has a smaller projection. Full relative means Asp-containing within the existing reduced selection boundary: internal Neg, Mood, Evid, Phas and comment operators remain excluded.
- Participant `-ou` abstracts the independently selected participant (the sole holder in underived State/Become); it does not automatically denote a state, transition event or result. Lexical/event `-ou` formations require their own construction and interpretation. Res requires a preceding event where lexical openness need not.
- Psychological other-person inferred experience favours the existing indirect evidential. Reporting, expressed feelings and narrative perspective remain possible; this is neither a categorical first-person restriction nor a rule for beauty, size, colour or observable bodily conditions.

A social classification alone does not guarantee that its holder can undergo the relevant scalar transition.
