Nouns, particles, and pronouns are entered in their direct or dictionary forms,
whereas verbal stems bear a following hyphen because they require overt
morphology. Entries give the inflected forms or lexical stem grades relevant to
their paradigms; full and bound stems belong to the same lexeme even where their
vowels differ in presence, height, or quantity. Nominal class labels are supplemented by
bound, palatal, plural, or oblique principal parts where
the citation form alone does not determine the paradigm
(§\,\ref{sec:nm-declension}). A recorded consonantal alternant does not
by itself identify its grammatical trigger: Class~I plural \ird{préš-}
and mixed-class \ird{študenc-} are stored principal
parts, whereas a productive PAL ending supplies the mapping at its licensed
boundary. The palatal stop [c], written \ird{tě} together with following
/ɛ/ and \ird{ť} elsewhere, remains distinct from the alveolar affricate
/ts/ written \ird{c}.

Lexical preverbs and their derivatives are described in
§\,\ref{sec:lexical-preverbs}. The derived motion stems entered below retain
the intransitive frame of their bases, with paths, sources, and goals expressed
through the ordinary dependent constructions. Prefixation itself licenses no
additional argument, and the construction-selected grades of each base remain
available; no exceptional alternants are specified for these derivatives. In the cognitive derivative
\ird{navad-}, the argument frame of \ird{vad-} is retained, although the
overt coding of separate considerations and the resulting judgement is not
independently specified.

Causative principal parts record the lexical grade of
§\,\ref{sec:causative-grades}; compact stems take expanded \ird{-ér-} when the local stem lacks
quantity, or \ird{-er-} after a quantity-bearing stem, where the
following suffixal environment requires it. Outer Voice, when overt,
adds \ird{-an-} after every rhotic causative grade, independently of
the lexical stem listed here.

These principal parts specify morphological formation, whose availability
is independent of a derivative's lexical and constructional licence
(§\,\ref{sec:causative-productivity}). In particular, a listed causative
grade does not license unrestricted causativisation of every sense of a
stem, nor does an antipassive alternant license patient suppression outside
the indicative ERG domain. The indicative NOM-domain activity uses of
\ird{pjašt-} and \ird{kař-} are separately restricted lexical frames
(§\,\ref{sec:nom-activity}); they do not supply bare intransitive
perfectives.

Quantity-class labels are independent of consonantal thematic classes.
Quantity Class~A selects root strength in the progressive and perfective
when the root is locally exposed;
Class~B selects strong aspect endings; Class~C preserves lexical root
quantity with short adjacent endings. Unless an entry specifies a stored
target, the eligible root nucleus is the rightmost non-fleeting one;
preverbs and inflectional exponents are outside that root target.
An intervening derivational head preserves its own quantity policy: additive
antipassive \ird{kanž-} and \ird{lovž-} remain short, while replacement
\ird{pjášč-} is strong. The irregular copular and existential principal parts remain separately
listed rather than being inferred from these productive classes.

\section{Lexical specification}

\subsection{Valency profiles}\label{sec:lexical-valency-profiles}

\ird{Pjašt-}, \ird{zemac-} and \ird{ščen-} illustrate distinct causative
inputs: a two-participant eating event, an actor's activity, and a change
involving a single arriving participant. Their listed causatives preserve
those differences (§\,\ref{sec:causative-productivity}). A valency profile
therefore specifies the basic participant frame and any independent
intransitive analysis, the licensed causative and applicative interpretations,
the conditions on causee expression, and the aspectual distribution of
patientless activity and antipassive frames. Allomorph selection is recorded
separately, since semantic licensing does not determine a rhotic grade.

An unresolved profile withholds a derivation without assigning it an
ungrammaticality mark. Conditional licences likewise state a requirement:
\ird{svorn-} needs a causable property interpretation, while a psychological
predicate such as \ird{všihn-} needs its individually specified causal
reading. The listed service use of \ird{mar-} closes the performer role
within the cooking event; it does not generalise to applied service
causatives or supply an independently recoverable causee
(§\,\ref{sec:causative-service}).

The listed noncausal--causal relations include \ird{ščen-}/\ird{ščenér-}
\trsl{arrive/cause to arrive}, \ird{zemac-}/\ird{zemácr-}
\trsl{run/cause to run}, and \ird{svorn-}/\ird{svornér-}
\trsl{be big/make bigger}. Causativisation of \ird{svornél-} embeds the
independently derived transition, rather than establishing an obligatory
STATE--BECOME sequence for every causal verb. Passive orientation preserves
a lexical event's agent role, whereas a decausative would remove external
causation from its lexical frame; neither passive \ird{-n-} nor reflexive
coreference licenses a general decausative alternation.

No lexical or suppletive \trsl{die/kill} opposition, or unrestricted
\trsl{break/open} alternation, is established by these families. The wider
inventory of spontaneous change, manipulation, transfer and bodily-state
pairs remains unspecified where the relevant stems or frames are unlisted.
Morphological transparency cannot fill those gaps, establish conventional
usage, or supply a frequency or register distinction. Complex events may
instead be distributed across independently licensed complement clauses
(§\,\ref{sec:complementation}); that alternative still requires a matrix
predicate with the relevant meaning and selectional frame.

Stem grades are recorded independently of spelling: \ird{perek} selects
\ird{pérk-}, while \ird{zděk-} selects raised-strong \ird{zďíčev} in the
irrealis and unraised \ird{zděcir} in the negative irrealis. Fleeting-vowel deletion and quantity selection are independent: bound
\ird{pérk-} has G1, while affirmative irrealis \ird{zďíč-} has G3.
Neither relation introduces a syllabic liquid.

The stems \ird{mulš-} and \ird{bydl-} occur with \trsl{live, dwell}.
Their distribution has not been established precisely enough to assign
distinct register or aspectual meanings.
