This chapter gathers lexical items directly attested in the current rewrite of
the grammar. Verbs are cited as stems with a trailing hyphen; nouns, particles,
and pronouns are cited in their direct or dictionary form. Variant forms are
given where the chapters explicitly discuss transitive or indirect case forms,
or where a root has a currently active palatalized or raised alternant.

\section{Current Conflicts}

The chapter drafts contain a number of lexical conflicts that should be
regularized before this appendix can be treated as stable. Items marked
\textbf{resolved} have been corrected in the vocabulary list above; items
marked \textbf{active} still require attention in the running text.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Status: resolved.} \textit{Indirect of \ird{lobek}.}
  The earlier lexicon listed both \ird{lubcení} and \ird{lubcí} as
  alternatives for the indirect of \ird{lobek} `apple'. The chapter
  text is now unanimous: Chapter~2 derives \ird{lubcí} explicitly via
  selection of the reduced raised bound stem and velar fronting; Chapters~4
  and~6 use it consistently in paradigm tables and clause examples. The
  form \ird{lubcení} is not attested anywhere in the current drafts.
  The lexicon entry has been updated to \ird{lubcí} only.

\item \textbf{Status: resolved.} \textit{Oblique paradigm of \ird{piaščikou}.}
  An earlier note flagged a tension between the anchoring discussion and
  the event-nominal paradigm in Chapter~4. The current text presents a
  consistent paradigm throughout: bare direct \ird{piaščikou}, anchored
  direct \ird{piaščikovat}, transitive with anchoring \ird{piaščikovyte},
  bare indirect \ird{piaščikovět}, and indirect with anchoring
  \ird{piaščikověte}. No inconsistency remains in the chapter text.

\item \textbf{Status: partially resolved.} \textit{\ird{hlavdy}/\ird{ktady}
  vs.\ \ird{izdy}/\ird{děne}.}
  Chapter~5 uses \ird{hlavdy} (inferential, `must') and \ird{ktady}
  (speculative, `perhaps') throughout, and the examples follow that
  system. The earlier forms \ird{izdy} and \ird{děne} survive only in
  historical commentary. They should remain quarantined to that context
  and not be reintroduced as active lexical entries.

\item \textbf{Status: bookkeeping.} \textit{Proximal demonstrative
  \ird{tak}/\ird{tam}/\ird{ten} vs.\ legacy \ird{ša}.}
  The productive demonstrative series in all current chapter text is
  \ird{tak}/\ird{tam}/\ird{ten}. The only surviving reference to
  \ird{ša} is an internal comment in Chapter~8. This is a stale data
  flag; \ird{ša} should not be reintroduced as an active entry.

\item \textbf{Status: resolved by lexicon correction.}
  \textit{\ird{stož-} vs.\ \ird{stoj-}.}
  The earlier lexicon carried \ird{stož-} as the stem for `go', but
  this form is unattested in any current chapter draft. Chapters~3
  and~7 consistently use \ird{stoj-} as the directed-motion verb, both
  in morphological analysis (e.g.\ the continuative-plural form
  \ird{stojách}) and in the purposive-motion construction. The lexicon
  entry has been corrected to \ird{stoj-}. Note that \ird{stoj-} and
  \ird{chod-} occupy distinct semantic niches: \ird{stoj-} encodes
  directed or goal-oriented motion and functions as the matrix verb in
  purposive constructions, while \ird{chod-} encodes habitual or
  multidirectional motion (cf.\ \ird{Marko domů chodime} `Marek goes
  home'). Both are now listed in the vocabulary.

\item \textbf{Status: active.} \textit{\ird{obroh-} vs.\ \ird{čit-} `read'.}
  Chapter~4 and early sections of Chapter~6 use \ird{obroh-} as the
  verb for `read' (attested: \ird{obrojime} read-\Cont{}, and the
  attributive nominal \ird{obrohnikovní} read-\Trs{}-\Pf{}-\textsc{attr}).
  The Chapter~6 §4 rewrite introduces \ird{čit-} in the same semantic
  role (attested: \ird{čitanik} read-\Trs{}-\Pf{}, \ird{čitajime}
  read-\Cont{}). Both stems now coexist in Chapter~6 without any
  stated distinction. It is not yet clear whether these are register
  variants, synonyms, or whether one represents a stale entry that
  should be retired. Both are retained in the vocabulary pending
  resolution; the chapters should be checked to determine whether
  \ird{obroh-} or \ird{čit-} is preferred for the base lexicon.

\item \textbf{Status: active.} \textit{\ird{mulš-} vs.\ \ird{bydl-}
  `live, dwell'.}
  The lexicon carries \ird{mulš-} `live, dwell', but this stem is
  not attested in any current chapter draft. Chapter~6 §4 uses
  \ird{bydl-} in the same semantic role (\ird{Marko v něm bydlime}
  `Marek already lives there'). Either \ird{bydl-} should be
  established as the primary entry and \ird{mulš-} retired, or their
  semantic or register distinction should be documented. Both are
  retained in the vocabulary for now; \ird{mulš-} should be treated
  as tentative pending attestation in the revised chapters.

\item \textbf{Status: active.} \textit{Indirect form of proper name
  \ird{Josif}.}
  Chapter~7 presents two inconsistent forms for the indirect of
  \ird{Josif}: the interlinear example \ref{ex:purp-meet} gives
  \ird{Josifam} (glossed \ird{Josif}.\Ind{}), while the immediately
  following prose parenthetical cites \ird{Josifi}. The \ird{-am}
  ending follows the Class~I pattern (cf.\ \ird{byl}~→~\ird{bylam}),
  while \ird{-i} suggests a Class~II or adjectival-stem treatment.
  The intended indirect form needs to be settled before \ird{Josif}
  can be added to the vocabulary with a paradigm.

\end{enumerate}
