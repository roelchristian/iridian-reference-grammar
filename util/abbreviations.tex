\section{Glossing abbreviations}
Interlinear examples distinguish lexical meanings from grammatical categories,
whose abbreviations are set in small capitals. A hyphen separates affixes from
their hosts, while a full stop separates grammatical values expressed by a
single form. Zero exponence is represented by $\varnothing$ where the analysis
requires an unpronounced exponent. Comparative examples retain the
case and tense labels of the language illustrated; these labels do not
identify additional Iridian inflectional categories
(§\,\ref{sec:lexical-slot-schemata}).

\section{Forms and analytical notation}
Iridian forms are italicised, translations appear in single quotation marks,
and phonetic transcriptions are enclosed in square brackets; angle brackets
identify orthographic representations. An asterisk marks an ungrammatical form
or, in an explicitly historical discussion, a reconstructed form.
Functional heads and projections retain their symbolic names; the inventory
below supplies their expansions and construction-specific uses. Nominal
inflection is treated in Chapter~\ref{ch:nominal-morphology} and anchoring
in Chapter~\ref{ch:nominal-syntax}.

\section{Syntactic symbols and labels}\label{sec:syntax-symbols}

This inventory covers the notation in the maintained chapters, including
prose, trees, structural schemata and their interpretation. It records the
analysis actually used here, rather than a universal inventory of possible
projections. The working hierarchies in §\,\ref{sec:grammatical-architecture}
and §\,\ref{sec:nm-funcseq} contain optional and construction-selected layers;
a label's inclusion does not require that layer in every expression.
Small-capital morpheme glosses in the preceding list are a separate system:
for example, \mk{state}, \mk{cop} and \mk{q} label morphological meanings,
not the projections StateP, EquP and PolP respectively. Capitalisation matters:
lexical N/V, categorising $n$/$v$, a quantifier Q and interrogative features
must not be identified merely because their names resemble one another.

QP & Quantifier phrase, used conservatively for the prenominal

establish two successive heads. \\
ATTR; AttrP & Attributive head/derivation and attributive phrase. Ordinary

$n_{\mathrm{part}}$ participant nominals. The last selects actual AspP and
abstracts over its privileged participant, without finite case valuation
(§\,\ref{sec:nominalised}). \\
Rel; RelP & Relative head and relative phrase. Reduced \ird{-ový} selects
AspP and supplies a participant property restricting the overt nominal head;
it excludes the higher finite operator layers (§\,\ref{sec:th-rel-structure}). \\
Post$^0$; PostP; PP & Postposition head and postpositional phrase; PP is the
shorter adpositional-phrase label used for locative/possessive adjuncts.

V; VP; $v$; vP & Verb head and verb phrase; verbal categoriser and its

State; StateP & State-forming head and state phrase. Introduces a state with

ResP & Result-state phrase. Used only where an independently licensed
predicate entails a preceding event and its state; it is not a universal
retrospective or lexical-state layer (§\,\ref{sec:vm-tension-stative}). \\
Voice; VoiceP & Voice head and phrase. Inner Voice hosts antipassive

predicate. AP means antipassive here, not adjective phrase
(§§\,\ref{sec:vm-root}, \ref{sec:vm-overview}). \\
Appl; ApplP & Applicative head and phrase. Adds/promotes the licensed applied
participant below Caus; $\mathrm{Appl}_{\mathrm{STIM}}$ names the
lexically restricted psychological stimulus extension, distinct from ordinary
benefactive application (§§\,\ref{sec:vm-appl}, \ref{sec:stative-valency}). \\
Caus; CausP & Causative head and phrase. Builds the higher causal predicate;
outer orientation and aspect evaluate the derived frame
(§\,\ref{sec:vm-caus}). \\
Asp; Asp.Align; AspP; Asp.AlignP & Aspect--alignment head and its projection.
AspP is the usual projection name; Asp.AlignP occurs in the stative
containment schema. The head combines viewpoint and alignment features;
finite case valuation is absent in participant nominals
(§§\,\ref{sec:vm-overview}, \ref{sec:vm-asp-case}). The isolated
Asp$^0\circ$Align$^0$ notation in §\,\ref{sec:purp-motion-structure}
is in tension with the explicitly single-head analysis; it does not establish
an additional AlignP in the adopted spine ($\circ$ marks composition there). \\
Neg; NegP & Negative head and phrase. Indicative Neg selects AspP or EquP;
its exponents are verbal \ird{za-}/\ird{z-}, equative \ird{staly}, or zero
conditioning existential root suppletion. Semantic negation belongs to
$\mathrm{Op}_{\neg}$ (§\,\ref{sec:vm-neg}). \\
Phas; PhasP & Phasal head/domain and phasal phrase. \ird{vy}/\ird{so}
evaluate polarised predication across stages, above indicative Neg
(§\,\ref{sec:class1-particles}). \\
Mood; MoodP & Mood head and phrase. Mood/finiteness, with null indicative
exponence and overt non-indicative suffixes. Negative Mood licenses a low
negative operator without separate NegP (§\,\ref{sec:vm-moodp}). \\
I; IP & Inflectional domain/phrase. Abbreviates the finite domain including
Mood and inner Evid when present; it asserts neither person agreement nor
a tense category (§\,\ref{sec:grammatical-architecture}). \\
T; T$'$; TP & Conventional tense-head/intermediate/phrase notation retained
for the embedded finite domain and conditional matrix schemata. Here TP
functions as the finite-clause label alongside IP, not evidence for an
independently established Iridian tense head
(§§\,\ref{sec:vm-tension-linker}, \ref{sec:cond-insub}). \\
Evid; EvidP & Evidential head/domain and phrase.
$\mathrm{EvidP}_{\mathrm{inner}}$ hosts verbal indirect \ird{-al}/\ird{-l};
$\mathrm{EvidP}_{\mathrm{outer}}$ covers evidential/epistemic particles above
PolP. Inner/outer are structural qualifiers, not interchangeable exponents
(§§\,\ref{sec:vm-evid}, \ref{sec:th-particle-framework}). \\
Pol; PolP & Interrogative clause-typing head and phrase. Locally head-initial
\ird{by} or its null counterpart; Pol does not name the indicative negation
head (§\,\ref{sec:by-pol-head}). \\
SpeechActP; SA$^0$ & Speech-act phrase and the abbreviated head label in the
insubordinate tree. Illocutionary/discourse framing, outside outer Evid
(§§\,\ref{sec:th-particle-framework}, \ref{sec:cond-insub}). \\
Ord; OrdP & Ordinary-placement head and phrase. Silent Ord orders eligible
arguments/modifiers above the particle field; it neither assigns topic status
nor values case (§\,\ref{sec:pivot-placement}). \\
Foc; FocP & Focus head and phrase. Marked focus or one marked wh-fronting
dependency, above OrdP (§\,\ref{sec:th-wh-interrogation}). \\
Top; TopP & Topic head and phrase. Optional linked aboutness topic, with case
retained; direct-case pivot selection alone does not place a DP here
(§\,\ref{sec:tc-marked-topics}). \\
Eq$^0$; Cop$^0$; EquP & Equative head (Eq in trees, Cop in the prose
analysis) and equative phrase. Licenses zero-copula nominal predication
without verbal AspP; overt finite \ird{neh-} instead projects VP
(§§\,\ref{sec:copula}, \ref{sec:neh}). \\
Exist$^0$; ExistP & Existential head and phrase. Introduces the direct-case
existential pivot; \ird{jec-}/\ird{zad-} alternate with the absence/presence
of NegP. ExistP is selected by Asp, and possessors/locations are PP adjuncts
(§\,\ref{sec:existence-and-presence}). \\
C; CP & Complementiser head and phrase. Head-final C selects the embedded
finite domain; \ird{po}/\ird{poby} and null C license clause-edge linker
\ird{-e} (§§\,\ref{sec:vm-tension-linker}, \ref{sec:vm-po-comp}). \\
Conv$^0$; ConvP; CondConvP & Converb head and phrase; conditional converb
phrase. Reduced verbal structure lacks finite Asp.Align and Mood;
CondConvP specifies the conditional subtype, not another finite projection
(§§\,\ref{sec:vm-purp-comp}, \ref{sec:cond-dm}). \\
Frame$^0$; FrameP & Null framing head and phrase in the adopted local analysis
of insubordinate conditionals. Selects CondConvP under SpeechActP without a
matrix consequent (§\,\ref{sec:cond-insub}). \\
\end{longtable}\endgroup

The Comm-field (also abbreviated Comm) is the descriptive region for
heterogeneous comment operators, not a single established comment phrase
(CommP) head or semantic type (§\,\ref{sec:particle-combinations}). PredP (predicate phrase) is explicitly
\emph{not} an additional projection in the verbal predicate domain
(§\,\ref{sec:grammatical-architecture}). Provisional
$pro_{\mathrm{POSS}}$ is a possible silent possessor, not a demonstrated
universal component of possessed nouns (§\,\ref{sec:nm-implicit-poss}).
Comparative analyses and their labels do not add Iridian projections merely
by being cited; the bounded uses of QP, ResP and FrameP above retain their
stated evidential limits.

(§§\,\ref{sec:nm-anchor-types}, \ref{sec:exst-quantification}). \\
