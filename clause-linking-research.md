# Clause-linking research record

Research checked on 31 October 2026. Comparative descriptions motivate analyses;
they do not supply evidence for Iridian forms or judgements. The live bibliography
is `util/references.tex` (included by `grammar.tex`), superseding the filename in
the local writing guidance. No clause-linking source below was already cited there
at the initial audit.

## Dixon and Aikhenvald volume: primary text read

R. M. W. Dixon and Alexandra Y. Aikhenvald (eds.). 2009. *The Semantics of
Clause Linking: A Cross-linguistic Typology*. Oxford University Press.
ISBN 9780199567225. [Primary-text university-hosted scan](https://coehuman.uodiyala.edu.iq/uploads/Coehuman%20library%20pdf/English%20library%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A8%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%83%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%B2%D9%8A/linguistics/The_Semantics_of_Clause_Linking.pdf).
Printed page numbers are 18 lower than zero-based PDF page indices.

- Ho-min Sohn, ‘The semantics of clause linking in Korean’, pp. 285–317.
  DOI: [10.1093/oso/9780199567225.003.0012](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199567225.003.0012).
  Checked §3, pp. 291–292: Types 1/2 distinguish conjunctive endings and
  conjunctive adverbials; anaphoric elements participate in the latter. These
  categories do not classify Iridian finiteness. Checked §4.1.1, pp. 300–302:
  *-ko* admits sequence, simultaneity and unordered addition, whereas *-ese/ase*
  requires a preconditional or causal relationship. Checked §4.1.3, pp. 303–306:
  conditional distribution depends on modality and consequent type. Checked
  §4.2, pp. 306–308, and §4.7, pp. 315–316: causal, temporal, result and means
  interpretations overlap; selected complements also use certain endings.
  §4.1.2, p. 303 distinguishes nominal/relative temporal constructions.
- Guy Deutscher, ‘The semantics of clause linking in Akkadian’, pp. 56–73.
  DOI: [10.1093/oso/9780199567225.003.0002](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199567225.003.0002).
  Checked §3, pp. 58–60: *-ma* relates finite main clauses, its specific relation
  depends on context, and ordinary linkage preserves temporal direction, with
  separately acknowledged pseudo-serial exceptions. It is not evidence that
  Iridian finite *-e* coordinates independent assertions.

## Contemporary primary scholarship

Soonja Choi. 2020. ‘Development of clause chaining in Korean’. *Frontiers in
Psychology* 11:256. [Full article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00256).
Read introduction sections ‘Topic Continuity and Subject Marking’ and
‘Finiteness Versus Non-finiteness of Medial Verbs’, results on continuity,
Table 5 and discussion. Korean connective functions show participant-continuity
preferences without an obligatory switch-reference marker; preferences can vary
with the function of a single form. Integration correlates with medial inflectional
independence. Acquisition and frequency findings concern the reported Korean
samples; they establish no Iridian corpus distribution or developmental history.

Jesús Olguín Martínez. 2023. ‘Temporal adverbial clauses: A cross-linguistic
perspective’. *Lingua Posnaniensis* 65(2):47–76.
[Publisher article](https://doi.org/10.14746/linpo.2023.65.2.2).
Read the [publisher PDF](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/linpo/article/download/40960/34303),
especially §4.1, pp. 60–61 (Tables 4–6), §4.2.4, pp. 69–70, and §4.2.5,
pp. 71–72.
The reported 218-language sample associates *after/before/until* particularly
with monofunctional markers, whereas *when* is especially polyfunctional.
Consequently, inventory reduction cannot be justified as a typological necessity.
The before/lest overlap provides an avertive comparison; until marks an endpoint,
from which purpose and result may be inferred. These patterns do not settle the
actuality or polarity profile of an Iridian endpoint construction.

## Phase II: semantic and causal distinctions

Sohn's §4.1.1, pp. 300–301 directly supplies the contrast between temporally
unspecified *-ko* and preconditional/causal *-ese/ase*. The Iridian reform preserves
completed anteriority for *-u* and adopts circumstantial relevance for *-ení*;
neither description is a translation of a Korean lexical entry. Choi's
participant-continuity findings support testing functional preferences separately
from categorical switch-reference requirements.

Eve Sweetser. 1990. *From Etymology to Pragmatics: Metaphorical and Cultural
Aspects of Semantic Structure*. Cambridge Studies in Linguistics 54. Cambridge
University Press. DOI: [10.1017/CBO9780511620904](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511620904).
Read the [publisher chapter extracts](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/from-etymology-to-pragmatics/F1454419128B2D24A372DD0808B51198/listing),
chapter 4, pp. 76–112, and chapter 5, pp. 113–144. Chapter 4 explicitly
distinguishes content, epistemic and conversational domains and pragmatic
application from lexical polysemy. Chapter 5 rejects treating natural-language
conditionals as merely truth-functional implication. Full chapters were not
accessible; cite chapter-level distinctions only, without unverified internal
page locators or examples. The grammar's separation of event causation,
inference and speech-act justification uses this distinction as an analytical
tool, not as evidence for additional Iridian suffixes.

## Phase III: actuality, prospective reference and concession

Toshiyuki Ogihara and Shane Steinert-Threlkeld. 2024. ‘Limitations of a modal
analysis of before and after’. *Semantics and Pragmatics* 17, article 1:1–21.
[Official final article](https://doi.org/10.3765/sp.17.1).
Read §1, especially pp. 1:2–1:3, and the statement of the analytical dispute.
Before-clauses include noncommittal readings (the reference event may or may
not occur) and anti-veridical readings (its occurrence is excluded by the
described circumstances). This supports treating actuality separately from
temporal order; it does not require adopting one contested formal analysis.
The Iridian nonactualising interpretation of *-esny* is a design specification,
whose compatibility with this comparison does not constitute attestation.

Sohn §4.1.3 p. 306 lists concessive conditionals; §4.4 p. 312 and §4.7 p. 315
show overlap between concession and contrast. The grammar should nevertheless
retain the Iridian requirement of a defeated expectation and distinguish an
asserted obstacle from an entertained condition. Korean conditional and purpose
distributions do not decide which Iridian matrix predicates select *-it*.

## Phase IV: temporal event nominals and polarity

Henriëtte de Swart, Jos Tellings and Bernhard Wälchli. 2022.
‘Not…Until across European languages: A parallel corpus study’.
*Languages* 7(1), article 56:1–33.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages7010056);
[institutional final PDF](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/150878073/languages_07_00056_v2.pdf).
Read §§1–2, pp. 1–6, especially §2.1 pp. 3–4. Durative *until*, punctual
predicates under negation, and asserted versus inferred positive post-phases
require separate analyses. The 21-language comparison shows that polarity and
connective selection interact; English *not until* is not a universal template.

The adopted Iridian design defaults temporal *z* to event completion and *do*
to onset, with an explicit contextual override; *na* requires overlap and *u*
contextual proximity. These are specifications of the reform, not findings
about Iridian inferred from scholarship. Endpoint *do* relates an extended
matrix interval to an identified actual or projected boundary; nominalisation
alone does not assert that a projected event actualises. Matrix negation over
the pre-boundary interval does not independently assert a subsequent positive
event. Negation of the whole interval claim remains a separate scope.

Olguín Martínez (2023), §4.1 pp. 60–61, supports the limited comparison of
monofunctional *after/before/until* with polyfunctional *when*. Neither that
distribution nor de Swart et al.'s findings fix the Iridian boundary defaults,
case selection, participant retention or suffix allomorphy.

Research assessment: the specified endpoint construction supplies the semantic
coverage needed to replace *-ezak* within this constructed-language revision.
Its constructed examples and English polarity stimuli demonstrate the design;
they are not elicited judgements or evidence of existing usage.

## Phase V: finite dependency and discourse anaphora

Holger Diessel and Merlijn Breunesse. 2020. ‘A typology of demonstrative
clause linkers’. In Åshild Næss, Anna Margetts and Yvonne Treis (eds.),
*Demonstratives in Discourse*, 305–340. Topics at the Grammar-Discourse
Interface 6. Berlin: Language Science Press.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.4055834);
[final institutional text](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/54343931/Diessel_and_Breunesse_2020_A_typology_of_demonstrative_clause_linkers.pdf).
Read §1, p. 306, distinguishing participant tracking from discourse-deictic
reference to clauses/propositions, and §6, pp. 321–325, on conjunctive adverbs.
The latter includes demonstrative–adposition combinations and constructions
with demonstratives and pro-verbs; Korean examples occur on p. 324. The source
motivates investigating constructional licensing before treating an individual
anaphor as a propositional connective. It supplies no Iridian exponent.
The institutional cover and actual chapter extent give 305–340; the chapter's
first-page self-citation gives 305–341 in error.

Sohn §3, pp. 291–292, classifies conjunctive endings, conjunctive adverbials
and unmarked apposition as Types 1–3. This is a classification of mechanisms,
not an Iridian finiteness or dependency taxonomy. Adverbials can also co-occur
with conjunctive endings inside one intonation unit (p. 290); Type 2 therefore
does not universally imply two independent utterances. Choi (2020), the
introduction's excluded-constructions discussion, does separately illustrate
independent sentences with a conjunctive adverb.

Deutscher §3, pp. 58–60, describes *-ma* attaching to a finite main clause,
with contextual interpretation constrained by temporal direction in ordinary
linkage. This comparison supports distinguishing contextual polysemy from
unrestricted addition. It does not turn dependent Iridian *-e* into independent
coordination, impose Akkadian temporal restrictions on every Iridian finite
chain, or infer independent assertion from overt finite morphology.
